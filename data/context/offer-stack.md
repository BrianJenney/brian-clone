# Parsity AI Engineering Program — Grand Slam Offer Stack

Objection → solution mapping for the AI Engineering program. Use for sales
conversations, objection handling, landing-page copy, and content angles.

## Credibility (the thing that makes every solution below believable)

Built and led production AI systems — including complex RAG pipelines and production agents — at two companies with very different products. Not theory, not a side project: shipped, in production, leading the build both times.

## Problem → Solution

**1. "I'm getting left behind, my skills are becoming obsolete"**
→ The curriculum is designed to keep evolving with the field, so staying enrolled/engaged is itself a way of staying current, not just a one-time catch-up.

**2. "This requires deep math / ML knowledge I don't have"**
→ For a full-stack dev, this isn't a full career pivot into ML — most open roles just have a specific skills gap on top of what you already do, not a demand for a data-science background. Career pivots are high-friction and risky; closing a gap is the lowest-friction path to the same relevance.

**3. "I'm a full-stack dev, no one will take me seriously as an ML engineer, and I don't want the stats/data-eng grind anyway"**
→ Not positioned as an ML engineer pivot at all. It's full-stack devs adding AI systems skills on top of what they already do — not a career change into a discipline they don't want.

**4. "I'm just using Claude/Cursor and calling it progress"**
→ Teaches the hard, unsexy stuff nobody else teaches — evals, context engineering, RAG pipelines, production agent architecture, and the tradeoffs between approaches. That's the gap between "using the tool" and "understanding the system."

**5. "I built a tutorial chatbot and don't really understand what I built"**
→ Capstone project built without training wheels — real understanding of production concepts, not tutorial-following. Can even be built from a real work use case.

**6. "I want to be part of the AI conversation at work, on the cool team"**
→ It's being able to speak intelligently about production, standards, and tradeoffs so you can sell the idea to leadership and your team lead in 1:1s. That's the actual skill that gets you "in the room."

**7. "I've heard of RAG, agents, context engineering but don't understand any of it"**
→ These are the literal curriculum — evals, context engineering, RAG pipelines, production agents — taught as the core, not a mention in passing.

**8. "I could probably figure out how to build an agent, but I don't know the pitfalls"**
→ Taught by someone who's hit those pitfalls in production twice, at two different companies — the tradeoffs and failure modes are the actual content, not an afterthought.

**9. "Boot camps suck and I don't want to be associated with one"**
→ Not run like a typical bootcamp. Backed by a U.S. Department of Labor grant — vetted by the federal government as a legitimate workforce development program, not "some bootcamp run by influencers." Taught by an engineer who's built production AI systems. Claude-certified partner, with a path to certification.

**10. "Will this actually get me hired? How do I prove it?"**
→ A recruiting-firm partnership gives direct visibility into what companies are actually hiring for right now — RAG, production-grade agents, context engineering, AI tooling. Not a guess, live market signal, and it's exactly what the curriculum teaches. Plus the capstone as tangible proof and the ability to speak fluently about production tradeoffs in interviews. Frame as "these are the in-demand skills" — never a placement promise.

**11. "I don't want to build another cookie-cutter project like everyone else"**
→ Capstone can be built from your actual work — several students have used real work projects, so the thing you show off is also directly usable on the job.

**12. "I have a full-time job, I won't be able to keep up"**
→ ~2–4 hours/week. Curriculum is text-based by design (easy to consume, easy to feed to an agent to study), built around concepts over grinding code — light footprint, not a second job.

**13. "I'll procrastinate / I won't finish"**
→ Cohort format creates forced accountability — you're in it with other people, on a schedule, not learning alone with no pressure.

**Bonus — "won't this just be outdated again in 6 months?"**
→ Curriculum is treated as living, not fixed — updated every cohort based on what's actually relevant (dropped fine-tuning when it stopped mattering, added MCP when it started). Guest speakers bring outside perspectives beyond just Brian's.

**Bonus — peer comparison / learning alone**
→ Cohort gives you a network of people to talk through ideas with in a low-stakes environment, not just solo study.
